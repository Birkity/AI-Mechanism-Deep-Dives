## Post 1/7

Raw agreement can look strong for the wrong reason.

If 92% of labels are "correct," two passes can agree a lot just by following the base rate.

Amir's benchmark: 91.7% agreement. Looks solid. But that number alone is incomplete.

## Post 2/7

Cohen's kappa asks a harder question:

"How much agreement remains after expected chance agreement?"

In Amir's case:

- observed agreement: 91.7%
- expected chance agreement: 84.4%
- kappa: 0.46

The 91.7% is only 7.3 points above what two random passes would produce from the marginal rates alone.

## Post 3/7

But kappa has its own trap.

When one label dominates, expected chance agreement becomes very high. That can depress kappa even when actual disagreement is small.

This is the kappa paradox: high raw agreement, moderate-looking kappa.

So which number is right?

## Post 4/7

Three metrics. Three readings of the same 91.7%:

- Cohen's kappa: 0.46 — chance from the product of skewed marginals
- PABAK: 0.83 — kappa under a 50/50 prevalence assumption
- Gwet's AC1: 0.90 — chance from average label proportions (more stable under high prevalence)

They disagree because they each model "chance" differently. The defensible report includes all three with interpretation, not just the one that looks best.

## Post 5/7

Also name the protocol correctly.

If the same rater labels the same tasks twice after 24 hours, that is intra-rater test-retest reliability — not inter-rater agreement.

Inter-rater requires independent raters. That distinction changes the claim: consistent for its author does not mean clear to anyone else.

## Post 6/7

There is also a threshold problem.

The benchmark currently requires >80% raw agreement per dimension.

But chance agreement here is 84.4%.

A threshold below chance is not a quality bar — any rubric passes it regardless of quality. The >80% floor needs to be raised above chance, or converted to a kappa requirement (κ ≥ 0.4 per dimension).

## Post 7/7

The full takeaway:

91.7% does not prove the rubric is perfect.

0.46 kappa does not prove it is broken.

Together with PABAK = 0.83, AC1 = 0.90, and a revised threshold, they say:

"Mostly consistent, high-prevalence labels. Report the bundle. Fix the floor."
